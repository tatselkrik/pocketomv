# Statistical methods

This guide describes pocketomv's statistical conventions and interpretation. All calculations run on the device.

## t-test effect sizes

Independent-samples Cohen's d uses the displayed first group minus second group. Student's test divides by the sample-size-weighted pooled SD. Welch's test divides by `sqrt((variance₁ + variance₂) / 2)`, the square root of the unweighted mean of the group variances, as in jamovi. These definitions differ when both sample sizes and variances are unequal. One-sample d divides the mean-minus-reference contrast by sample SD; paired dᶻ divides the mean paired difference by the SD of those differences. No small-sample bias correction is applied.

## t-test confidence intervals

One-sample, independent and paired t tests use 95% confidence intervals consistent with the selected hypothesis. Two-sided alternatives use the .975 t quantile. A greater-than alternative uses the .95 quantile for a finite lower bound and an unbounded upper end; a less-than alternative uses an unbounded lower end and a finite upper bound. The one-sample results show both the population mean interval and the mean-minus-reference interval. Regression coefficient intervals and pairwise comparisons keep their separately stated two-sided definitions.

## Mann–Whitney U

The main statistic is `U = min(U₁, U₂)`, matching jamovi's reporting convention. The estimates retain U₁ for the displayed first group and U₂ for the second, with `U₁ + U₂ = n₁ × n₂`. Swapping the groups exchanges U₁ and U₂ while preserving the smaller U and the two-sided p-value.

Directional alternatives still test the first group relative to the second. Their p-values use the original group order, independently of which U is smaller. The headline convention applies to the Results screen and text, HTML and PDF reports; method notes identify both groups and the selected alternative.

## Normality

Shapiro–Wilk tests are available for one-sample, independent-samples and paired-samples t tests, one-way ANOVA and multiple-regression residuals. The one-sample check uses the selected sample. New independent-samples requests use pooled group-centered residuals. One-way ANOVA uses standardized model residuals, dividing each group-centered value by `sqrt(1 − 1/group N)`; the common residual-SD scale cancels in Shapiro–Wilk. These match the distinct jamovi module conventions. Singleton-group standardized residuals are undefined and omitted with a note, while the main ordinary ANOVA retains the observations; jamovi's one-way module can reject singleton groups entirely. Separate-group checks or both views are selectable, with each analysis retaining its own setting. Older saved sessions retain their previous separate-group settings. The paired check uses complete-pair differences. Regression checks fitted-model residuals. Normality selection does not change the main test or independently selected graphs.

The statistic and p-value follow Royston's AS R94 approximation, with the exact small-sample transformation for three observations. Supported sample size is 3–5,000. Constant samples and unsupported sample sizes produce an explanation instead of an invented result. Group normality p-values are unadjusted diagnostic values.

Values are normalized before calculation to limit loss of precision at very large or small scales. Normal quantiles and tail probabilities use Apache Commons Statistics. The coefficient and p-value approximations adapt SciPy's implementation; its [BSD license is included in the APK](../app/src/main/assets/third-party/scipy-LICENSE.txt).

## Homogeneity of variances

Independent-samples t tests and one-way ANOVA default to mean-centered Levene, following jamovi. A median-centered Brown–Forsythe variant remains selectable, and older saved sessions retain that median setting. Both apply a one-way F test to absolute distances from each group's chosen center. Their degrees of freedom are the number of groups minus one, and the included sample size minus the number of groups. Group origins are removed before common scaling to preserve small variation under large group offsets. Fixed-effects models retain their separately described homogeneity calculation.

Each group needs at least two observations, with positive variation in absolute deviations within the groups collectively. An undefined check is reported as unavailable, while the valid main analysis remains visible.

A small diagnostic p-value is evidence against the tested assumption. A nonsignificant value does not establish that the assumption holds, especially in small samples. Independence is a property of the study design and cannot be established from these tests. These checks do not automatically select Student, Welch, a rank test or another analysis.

## ANOVA follow-up comparisons

Enable pairwise comparisons to compare every distinct pair of groups. The existing variance selection determines the method:

| Main analysis | Follow-up method |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary ANOVA, equal variances | Tukey–Kramer |
| Welch ANOVA, unequal variances | Games–Howell |

Every row shows first group minus second group, its standard error, signed t statistic, degrees of freedom, adjusted two-sided p-value and a 95% interval using the same family adjustment. Tukey–Kramer uses the common ANOVA residual mean square; Games–Howell uses the two group variance estimates and Welch–Satterthwaite degrees of freedom. The interval multiplier is the 95th percentile of the studentized range divided by the square root of two. Games–Howell is an approximate procedure. Tukey–Kramer is exact for balanced normal, equal-variance groups and conservative for unequal sample sizes under those assumptions.

Adjusted probabilities account for the complete family of group pairs. Requested comparisons are displayed independently of the omnibus p-value. The screen shows up to 100 comparisons; a saved or copied text report includes every pair, up to the existing 40-group limit.

Adjusted probabilities and intervals use the studentized-range distribution. With two groups, the calculation reduces to the two-sided t distribution.

## Graphs

Graph options start unchecked. Graphs use the same selected values and missing-row rules as the main analysis. Descriptives weights affect only the weighted mean, so graphs show unweighted observations. Paired boxplots use both measurements from complete pairs; the paired t-test Q–Q plot uses first measurement minus second measurement. Wilcoxon boxplots retain complete pairs with zero differences, which are omitted only from the test's ranks.

### Boxplots

Quartiles use R type 7 linear interpolation, matching the app's descriptive percentiles. The box spans Q1 to Q3, with a line at the median. Whiskers extend to the most extreme observations within Q1 minus 1.5 IQR and Q3 plus 1.5 IQR, including values exactly on a fence. Points outside those fences are marked as outliers and remain in all calculations. These quartiles can differ from software using Tukey hinges for small samples.

Independent groups share a value axis and retain the analysis's group order. Sideways scrolling preserves readable group labels; the summary table includes full names, sample sizes, quartiles, whiskers and outlier counts. Constant samples collapse to a line.

### Normal Q–Q plots

Theoretical normal quantiles use the R `qqnorm`/`ppoints` convention: probability `(i − a) / (n + 1 − 2a)`, with i from 1 to n, a = 3/8 for n at most 10 and a = 1/2 otherwise. Sorted observed values are plotted against those quantiles. General sample plots use a reference line through the theoretical normal and observed R type 7 quartiles, matching the default `qqline` convention. Multiple-regression and standardized ANOVA/ANCOVA residual plots instead use the identity line with their respective residual standardizations described below.

Each independent group has its own selectable plot. Fewer than three observations displays an explanation; constant samples plot as a horizontal line with a warning that normality cannot be assessed. Q–Q plots can display all 20,000 supported rows, independently of the Shapiro–Wilk sample-size limit. A roughly straight pattern supports compatibility with a normal distribution but does not establish normality or independence.

### Scatterplots

The first variable is X and the second is Y. Every complete pair is plotted, using original values for Pearson and Spearman. The regression plot adds the fitted least-squares line, computed in normalized coordinates to avoid overflow. No random sampling or jitter is applied; identical pairs share a marker. Axis normalization also handles finite data whose unscaled range would overflow.

Figures are displayed on screen and embedded as vectors in PDF/HTML reports. Text reports contain numerical summaries. Formatted reports include every requested Q–Q group, all points and complete frequency tables. Multiple-regression Q–Q plots use sample-standardized residuals and an identity line, while residuals-versus-fitted plots retain original units. Repeated-measures boxes use the complete participants included in the analysis. Plot settings and the selected Q–Q group survive reopening.

## Reliability

Select 2–60 numeric items and at least three complete participants. Missing rows are excluded listwise across selected items only. Alpha is `k / (k − 1) × (1 − sum(item variances) / variance(item sum))`; standardized alpha uses the correlation matrix. Negative alpha remains negative. Zero total variation makes raw alpha unavailable. Standardized alpha is unavailable when any selected item is constant. Item-rest correlations exclude that item from the raw total; alpha if removed uses the same complete participants rather than a different sample for each row.

Omega fits one congeneric factor by minimum residuals (MINRES) to the item correlation matrix, with standardized loading λ and uniqueness `1 − λ²`. The standardized coefficient is `1 − sum(uniqueness) / sum(observed correlations)`, following the correlation-based omega-total definition used by jamovi. Raw omega uses `1 − sum(item variance × uniqueness) / variance(observed item sum)`. The denominator retains observed residual covariances rather than replacing them with the fitted model's variance. The fit directly minimizes off-diagonal squared correlation residuals and accepts only interior solutions with every uniqueness greater than 0.005.

Omega requires at least three varying items, a positive-definite correlation matrix, a converged interior solution and at least three nontrivial loadings. Loadings and omega may be negative. A global factor-sign orientation does not reverse any item. Unavailable estimates carry a reason; singular and boundary models are rejected even if a reference program returns a coefficient. Numerical optimization stopping criteria can produce small differences in unrounded values. Alpha and omega do not establish validity, dimensionality or suitability for a diagnosis. No automatic cutoff or item removal is applied.

- [jamovi reliability calculations](https://github.com/jamovi/jmv/blob/master/R/reliability.b.R)
- [psych omega and observed-variance definition](https://personality-project.org/r/psych/help/omega.html)

## Multiple linear regression

The model is ordinary least squares with an intercept and 1–12 simultaneous quantitative predictors, using complete observations across outcome and predictors. There must be more than `predictors + 1` complete observations. Constant variables and nearly redundant predictor columns are rejected. Coefficients and fitted/residual values return to original units.

Residual degrees of freedom are `n − predictors − 1`. RMSE is `sqrt(residual sum of squares / n)`, following jamovi. The separately reported residual standard error is `sqrt(residual sum of squares / residual df)` and supplies the variance estimate for coefficient inference. Simple regression also reports both definitions. Other statistics include R², adjusted R², overall F, unstandardized B, standard errors from the inverse design Gram matrix, standardized β, two-sided t tests and 95% t intervals. VIF is the diagonal of the inverse predictor correlation matrix; tolerance is its reciprocal. Numerically perfect fits have no usable residual variation, so coefficient tests and intervals are unavailable. Their F is infinite and this condition is explained.

Optional Shapiro–Wilk and Q–Q plots diagnose residual distribution; the residuals-versus-fitted plot supports inspecting functional form and spread. The Q–Q plot centers residuals and divides by their sample standard deviation, `z = (e − mean(e)) / sd(e)` with SD divisor `N − 1`, then uses normal ppoints and the identity reference line `y = x`, matching jamovi's multiple-regression module. This uses neither residual degrees of freedom nor a leverage adjustment in the plot standardization. Outcome units therefore do not change the Q–Q scale. A perfect fit has no usable residual SD and displays an unavailable-plot explanation. Coefficient inference, Shapiro–Wilk and residuals-versus-fitted values retain their existing calculation. Diagnostics do not establish independence, equal variance, absence of influential cases or causal effects. Categorical predictors need indicator coding with a reference category; automatic coding, interaction generation and model selection are not performed.

- [R linear-model summaries](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/stats/html/summary.lm.html)

## Repeated-measures ANOVA

One within-subject factor uses 2–12 condition columns in wide format and at least three complete participants. Participant identifiers are not conditions. All selected conditions and paired follow-ups use the same listwise sample. The condition sum of squares is `n × sum((condition mean − grand mean)²)`. Error removes participant and condition means. F divides condition MS by within-subject error MS, with uncorrected df `k − 1` and `(n − 1)(k − 1)`. Partial eta-squared is condition SS divided by condition SS plus error SS.

Orthonormal contrasts supply the within-subject covariance. Greenhouse–Geisser epsilon is squared trace divided by `(k − 1) × trace(covariance²)`, bounded from `1/(k − 1)` to 1. Huynh–Feldt is `(n(k − 1)εGG − 2) / ((k − 1)(n − 1 − (k − 1)εGG))`, capped at 1; an undefined estimate is unavailable. Corrections multiply both df by epsilon and leave F unchanged. The default is GG; a diagnostic never silently changes the selected correction.

Mauchly's W is the determinant of contrast covariance divided by its mean eigenvalue raised to `k − 1`. Its chi-square approximation uses the small-sample correction and second-order probability correction used in R's `mauchly.test`. Singular covariance or too few participants makes the diagnostic unavailable. Sphericity holds automatically for two conditions. A nonsignificant diagnostic does not prove sphericity. Participants must be independent and within-subject contrasts suitably distributed. This model does not include between-subject factors, multiple repeated factors, interactions or mixed effects.

Optional follow-ups are paired two-sided t tests. Holm-adjusted p-values control the planned pairwise family; Bonferroni 95% family confidence intervals use a different adjustment and are labeled separately. Zero-variance differences have no test or interval; those comparisons remain in the planned family count. Follow-ups are displayed independently of the omnibus p-value.

## Grouped descriptives and distribution shape

Select one or more variables and optionally one separate grouping column. Numeric or categorical interpretation applies to every selected variable. Each variable/group summary uses its own available observations. Missing values in another selected variable do not remove its valid observations. Blank group labels are excluded; literal `NA` group labels remain categories. Group order is alphabetical by stored label. An empty variable/group remains visible with N = 0 and unavailable statistics.

Grouped frequency tables use each variable's pooled valid N across nonblank groups for the primary percentage column, following jamovi. A separate column gives within-group percentages; an empty group's within-group percentage is unavailable. Category levels are shared across groups, including zero counts and levels found only in excluded blank-group rows. To bound memory, a frequency grid above 200,000 cells retains every observed category row and pooled percentage while explicitly omitting zero-count rows. Bar charts use counts and skip groups with no valid observations. Ungrouped percentages continue to use the selected variable's valid N. Frequencies and graphs are unweighted.

Mode is the first value in sorted order among those with the highest count, following jamovi's numeric mode rule. Numeric values are ordered numerically; category labels are ordered alphabetically. All-distinct and uniformly frequent samples still have a displayed mode under this convention. The complete tied-mode set is retained; long displayed lists refer to the frequency table instead of repeating thousands of values. Empty groups have no mode. Category modes are an additional summary, rather than a claimed equivalent to jamovi's unavailable nominal-mode output.

Let `m_j` be the jth central moment with divisor n. Adjusted sample skewness is `sqrt(n(n−1))/(n−2) × m_3/m_2^(3/2)`. Bias-corrected sample excess kurtosis is `(n−1)/((n−2)(n−3)) × ((n+1)(m_4/m_2² − 3) + 6)`. Skewness needs n ≥ 3, kurtosis n ≥ 4, and both need nonconstant values. The normal-distribution reference for excess kurtosis is zero. These are unweighted sample measures, independent of the sample/population variance and SD choice. See the [SciPy skewness](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.skew.html) and [kurtosis definitions](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.kurtosis.html).

Weighted means use only complete, positive-weight observations for the particular variable/group. A group with no positive weights retains its unweighted statistics with an explanation for the unavailable weighted mean. Histograms, percentiles, boxplots and Q–Q plots use that summary's unweighted values. Categorical bar charts use non-blank category counts, with a zero baseline and a common count scale within each summary. Charts show at most the first 20 categories in label order; frequency tables retain every category.

## Correlation matrices

Pearson r uses centered numeric values. Spearman rho correlates average ranks, recalculated after the pair's missing observations are removed. Pairwise deletion determines a sample for each pair; listwise deletion restricts every pair to the same rows complete across all selected variables. Unselected columns do not affect inclusion. Every matrix includes sample sizes. A pairwise matrix need not be positive semidefinite and should not automatically be used as a factor-analysis input.

Coefficients require at least two complete observations and variation in both variables. Pearson inference requires at least three: `t = r sqrt((n−2)/(1−r²))` with n − 2 degrees of freedom, tested two-sided. Spearman inference supports two observations and follows the R 4.5.0 rules: exact rank-permutation probabilities for untied N ≤ 9, the AS 89 Edgeworth approximation for untied N = 10–1,290, and the t approximation when ties occur or N > 1,290. Ties are assessed separately after each pair's missing observations are removed. No continuity correction is applied. Exact directional probabilities include the observed rank arrangement; exact two-sided p-values double the relevant tail and are capped at one. The displayed N − 2 df describe only the t approximation. See [R's correlation-test definitions](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/stats/html/cor.test.html) and [Best and Roberts' AS 89](https://doi.org/10.2307/2347111). P-values are unadjusted for testing multiple correlations. Constant variables and insufficient samples produce explained unavailable cells rather than zeros. Self-correlations have no p-value or interval.

Optional Pearson 95% intervals transform with `z = atanh(r)` and return `tanh(z ± Φ⁻¹(.975)/sqrt(n−3))`. They require at least four complete observations and use a large-sample approximation under independent, bivariate-normal sampling. At a computed coefficient of ±1 they collapse to that endpoint. Spearman intervals are not provided. See [Fisher-transform confidence intervals](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats._result_classes.PearsonRResult.confidence_interval.html) and [jamovi's correlation-matrix options](https://docs.jamovi.org/jmv/jmv_corrMatrix.html). Correlation alone does not establish causation.

## Fisher's exact test and association measures

For frequency-count input, new requests require a complete count column, following jamovi. A missing count is unknown, not zero; fill it in or explicitly select **Omit rows with missing counts**. The omission option excludes those rows and labels the policy in reports. Older saved sessions retain omission when the policy field is absent. Blank category labels remain excluded, while literal `NA` category labels remain categories. Unselected columns do not affect inclusion.

To bound exact-test runtime on a phone, the fixed margins may define at most 100,000 feasible tables: `1 + min(row total 1, row total 2, column total 1, column total 2)`. This limit is separate from the total-frequency limit. Very large totals can remain usable with narrow margins. Calculations outside either limit are rejected with an explanation; no approximate p-value is silently substituted.

Fisher's exact test supports a 2 × 2 table with positive row and column margins and a total frequency no greater than a signed 32-bit integer. Complete raw observations and collapsed whole-number frequencies share the same table calculations; zero-only categories do not expand the table. The test conditions on both margins. For a two-sided alternative, the p-value sums possible tables with probability no greater than the observed table. Directional alternatives use the corresponding hypergeometric tail. See [Apache Commons Statistics' exact-test definition](https://commons.apache.org/proper/commons-statistics/commons-statistics-inference/apidocs/org/apache/commons/statistics/inference/FisherExactTest.html).

The displayed sample odds ratio is `ad/bc`, rather than a conditional maximum-likelihood estimate. In the oriented table, `a` and `b` are event/non-event counts for the selected comparison group; `c` and `d` are event/non-event counts for the other, reference group. Relative risk is `[a/(a+b)]/[c/(c+d)]`. Reversing a group or event choice changes interpretation. Phi is the nonnegative magnitude, following jamovi. The separately labeled signed Phi is `(ad−bc)/sqrt((a+b)(c+d)(a+c)(b+d))` and retains orientation information. Cramér's V is `sqrt(chi-square/[N min(r−1,c−1)])`, using the uncorrected Pearson statistic; it also remains available for larger tables.

Approximate odds-ratio intervals exponentiate `log(OR) ± z(.975) sqrt(1/a+1/b+1/c+1/d)`. If any cell is zero, the odds-ratio measure adds 0.5 to all four cells before calculating the point estimate and interval, following jamovi. Its note identifies this adjustment, and a separate uncorrected sample row retains the original zero/infinite boundary. Fisher's exact p-value uses the original table and its sample odds-ratio statistic stays uncorrected. Katz relative-risk intervals exponentiate `log(RR) ± z(.975) sqrt(1/a−1/(a+b)+1/c−1/(c+d))`, with positive event counts in both groups; relative risks are not corrected. Unavailable intervals are explained. These intervals are large-sample approximations even when the hypothesis-test p-value is exact. See [sample versus conditional odds ratios](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.contingency.odds_ratio.html) and [relative-risk orientation and intervals](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.contingency.relative_risk.html). Interpret relative risks only when the sampling design supports comparing group event probabilities; association does not establish causation.

## Friedman and follow-up comparisons

Arrange 3–12 repeated conditions in wide format, with one row per participant and at least two complete participants. All selected conditions share one listwise complete sample, including summaries, mean ranks, plots and follow-ups. Rank each participant's conditions from low to high, assigning average ranks to ties. With rank sum `R_j`, participant count N and condition count k, the uncorrected statistic is `12 sum((R_j−N(k+1)/2)²)/(Nk(k+1))`. Divide it by `C = 1−sum(t³−t)/(N(k³−k))`, summing tied-run sizes t within each participant. The p-value uses a chi-square approximation with k−1 degrees of freedom. All-within-participant ties make the test undefined. Kendall's W is the corrected statistic divided by `N(k−1)`.

Durbin–Conover follow-ups use condition mean-rank differences and shared residual rank variance. If `r_ij` is a participant/condition rank, let `SSE = sum((r_ij−meanRank_j)²)` and `df = (N−1)(k−1)`. The standard error for a mean-rank difference is `sqrt(2 SSE/(N df))`; its t statistic has df degrees of freedom. Zero residual rank variance makes these follow-up t tests unavailable. P-values are two-sided. New requests use unadjusted probabilities, following jamovi. Holm and Bonferroni adjustments across all planned condition pairs remain selectable; older saved sessions retain Holm when their correction field is absent. Every PDF section repeats both condition labels when a pair table is split into column groups.

The chi-square approximation can be inaccurate with small samples or few conditions. Participants must be independent, and measurements must permit meaningful within-participant ranking. A median/shift interpretation needs an appropriate model; nonparametric tests still have assumptions. Follow-ups are displayed independently of the omnibus p-value. See [NIST's Friedman and multiple-comparison definitions](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman1/auxillar/friedman.htm), [SciPy's tied-rank statistic and approximation limits](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.friedmanchisquare.html), and [jamovi's Friedman options](https://docs.jamovi.org/jmv/jmv_anovaRMNP.html).

- [R Mauchly sphericity test](https://github.com/wch/r-source/blob/trunk/src/library/stats/R/mlm.R)
- [Pingouin sphericity and epsilon formulas](https://github.com/raphaelvallat/pingouin/blob/master/src/pingouin/distribution.py)
- [R multiple-testing adjustments](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/stats/html/p.adjust.html)

## Factorial ANOVA and ANCOVA

Fixed-effects ordinary least squares fits a numeric outcome, an intercept and sum-to-zero factor contrasts. Factorial ANOVA accepts 2–3 categorical factors; ANCOVA accepts 1–3 factors and 1–6 quantitative covariates. Factor values, including numeric labels, define categories. Each factor needs 2–6 observed levels. Every term, mean, comparison and diagnostic uses the same complete-case sample. Covariates are centered at their complete-case means and enter with common linear slopes. Factor interactions are either all included, through the three-way term, or all omitted for an additive model.

The full design must be of full column rank, with more complete observations than parameters and usable residual variation. Empty cells in an interaction model, confounding, duplicate predictors and constant covariates may prevent fitting; no variable is silently dropped. Limits are 60 combinations of factor levels and 60 parameters. A calculation budget also bounds repeated model fits: `N × parameters² × (term count + 2) ≤ 150,000,000`, with each individual fit limited to `N × parameters² ≤ 40,000,000`.

The headline F compares the full model with an intercept-only model. The term table shows sums of squares, degrees of freedom, mean squares, F, p and partial η². All term F tests use the full model's residual mean square. Partial η² is `term SS / (term SS + residual SS)` for the selected sums-of-squares convention.

- **Type III (default):** test each term after all other terms, using sum contrasts. Main effects in an interaction model describe the corresponding averaged contrasts.
- **Type II:** test each term after terms that do not contain it as a higher-order relative. This respects the model hierarchy; interpret interactions before lower-order terms.
- **Type I:** add terms sequentially in the table's order: factor main effects, covariates, then interactions by order. Unbalanced designs can give different answers when factor or covariate order changes. Selection follows worksheet column order.

Types II/III term sums of squares need not add to the total. Residual sums, model R² and adjusted R² refer to the fitted full model regardless of term-test convention.

Estimated marginal means average predictions with equal weights across every combination of the other factor levels, with covariates held at their means. Their 95% t intervals are pointwise. Pairwise contrasts compare levels of each factor in that same marginal reference grid. Two-sided t tests use the full residual degrees of freedom. New requests default to Tukey p-values within each factor, following jamovi: evaluate the studentized-range survival probability at `sqrt(2) × abs(t)` using that factor's level count and the full residual df. Two-level comparisons reduce to the two-sided t test. Multilevel Tukey probabilities are unavailable with residual df below two under the reference convention, while raw probabilities and the model remain available.

Holm, Bonferroni and unadjusted probabilities remain selectable, with separate per-factor families or one family across all displayed factors. Tukey supports per-factor families only. Older saved sessions retain their correction and global-family choices; a missing legacy correction field restores Holm. These are comparisons of adjusted marginal means, not automatic simple-effects tests within individual cells.

Optional Shapiro–Wilk checks use raw model residuals. New factorial ANOVA/ANCOVA Q–Q plots use internally standardized residuals `e / (residual SD × sqrt(1 − leverage))`, normal ppoints and the identity line `y = x`, matching jamovi. Undefined unit-leverage residuals are omitted from this plot only. With residual df1, two finite standardized residuals may remain; their plot has very little information. The raw-residual/quartile-line alternative stays selectable and is restored for older sessions without the basis setting. Residuals-versus-fitted plots remain in original units. New homogeneity checks apply a one-way ANOVA to the absolute fitted-model residuals across observed factor cells, following jamovi. Covariate effects remain in those residuals; they are not centered again within cells. A separate raw-cell median Levene option remains available, and older saved sessions retain that option. Both use the main model's complete observations. Unusable within-cell variation in absolute residuals makes the diagnostic unavailable while preserving the main model. The optional ANCOVA slope check jointly adds each fitted factor term × every covariate and compares the expanded model with the common-slope model. It uses the expanded model's residual variance and degrees of freedom. If that model is too large, redundant or lacks residual variation, the slope check is unavailable while the main result remains. It does not automatically change the main fitted model. Diagnostics cannot establish independence, causality, a correct mean model or that a nonsignificant assumption is satisfied.

See [jamovi's factorial ANOVA options](https://docs.jamovi.org/jmv/jmv_ANOVA.html), [ANCOVA options](https://docs.jamovi.org/jmv/jmv_ancova.html) and [statsmodels' sums-of-squares definitions](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/generated/statsmodels.stats.anova.anova_lm.html). Available options and statistical conventions are specified here; support does not imply identical results for every jamovi configuration.

## Exploratory factor analysis

EFA fits a common-factor model to the Pearson correlation matrix of 3–20 varying numeric items. It uses listwise complete observations, with `N ≥ max(10, items + 1)`. This minimum is a calculation limit, not evidence of adequate sampling. Constant items and singular or nearly singular correlation matrices are rejected. Ordinal/polychoric analysis, factor scores and maximum-likelihood fit tests are not included.

**MINRES** uses leading common-factor eigenvectors after replacing each correlation diagonal with `1 − uniqueness`, with uniqueness parameters constrained to [0.005, 1]. New requests use off-diagonal residual loss, the diagonal fixed-point updating direction, squared-multiple-correlation starts and bounded limited-memory BFGS with the reference optimizer's scaling and stopping conventions. The controls are parameter scale 0.01, memory 5, relative-objective factor 1e7 and at most 100 iterations. Numerical stopping is part of the convention; it need not give the smallest attainable residual loss. The selectable **Tight spectral least squares** alternative retains the previous full residual-matrix objective, logistic bounds, multiple starts and tighter gradient stopping. Older sessions retain that alternative. A uniqueness at or near the lower bound is noted.

**Principal axis factoring** starts from squared multiple correlations. New requests use undamped communality updates, stopping when the absolute change in total communality is at most 0.001, with a 50-iteration limit, following jamovi. Tight convergence remains selectable: damped updates until every individual change is below 1e-8, at most 1,000 iterations. Older saved sessions retain this tight rule. Both methods estimate common factors; PCA loadings are not substituted. Nonconvergence and zero/negative uniqueness produce an explanation rather than an accepted solution. See the [bounded optimization algorithm](https://users.iems.northwestern.edu/~nocedal/lbfgsb.html) and [R optimizer control definitions](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-patched/library/stats/html/optim.html).

**Oblimin** is the default rotation for new analyses: unnormalized quartimin (gamma 0), identity transformation, no random starts, projected-gradient norm tolerance `1e-5` and a 1,000-iteration limit. Pattern is `A × inverse(T) transpose`; factor correlations are `T transpose × T` with unit-length transformation columns. Each step projects onto that constraint and uses up to twelve backtracking trials. Singular transformations, unsuccessful line searches and unconverged solutions are unavailable, with no automatic rotation substitute. Zero loading rows do not require Kaiser normalization. A stationary solution need not be a global minimum; different starts or tolerance choices can yield different results. Older sessions retain their saved rotation, or Varimax when the field is absent. The matrix calculus follows [Jennrich (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02294706) and [Bernaards and Jennrich (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1177/0013164404272507).

**Varimax** uses Kaiser row normalization. Its default polar iteration stops at relative singular-sum change `1e-5`, with at most 1,000 iterations. Tight pairwise plane rotation remains selectable, using angle tolerance `1e-7` and at most 250 sweeps; older saved sessions retain that choice. A stationary polar solution can fail to maximize the criterion on symmetric inputs. Signs and factor order are arbitrary, while distinct stationary solutions can have materially different loadings. **Promax** starts from the selected Kaiser-normalized Varimax solution and a power-4 target. The default uses polar stopping; older saved sessions retain tight plane rotation. It permits correlated factors and reports pattern coefficients, structure correlations and factor correlations. Structure is `pattern × factor correlations`; communality is the diagonal of `pattern × factor correlations × pattern transpose`. Uniqueness is one minus communality. With no rotation or Varimax the factor correlation matrix is the identity. Factor signs and ordering are standardized for readability. The loading threshold affects display only. The common-variance summary describes the orthogonal extraction before rotation; correlated factors are not independent portions of explained variance.

Factor count can be fixed, chosen by an eigenvalue rule, or suggested by parallel analysis. The eigenvalue rule defaults to common-factor eigenvalues from a preliminary one-factor fit with the selected extraction and stopping rule. MINRES replaces the correlation diagonal with that fit's communalities; principal axis uses the final reduced matrix before its last communality update. These are not PCA variances and can be negative. Retain values strictly greater than the chosen nonnegative threshold. The threshold starts at 0, the jmv 2.8.0 EFA option default; choose 1 explicitly for a threshold of 1. The correlation/PCA alternative remains selectable, and older sessions retain it and their prior threshold. Preliminary nonconvergence or improper communalities make that spectrum provisional, with a note.

Parallel analysis defaults to 20 independent Gaussian null datasets with the same complete N and variable count, using the selected extraction and fit stopping. It compares ordered observed common-factor eigenvalues with the corresponding 95th-percentile null thresholds, stopping at the first failed comparison and suggesting at least one factor, following jamovi. That minimum does not establish a usable factor solution; ordinary extraction guards still apply. A visible seed defaults to 20261109. The Mersenne-Twister/Inversion stream is compatible with R's signed integer seed convention. Match both seed and RNG kind for reproducible comparisons; an unseeded jamovi session uses its ambient random state and can differ. See [R random-number generation and reproducibility](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-patched/library/base/html/Random.html).

The selectable PCA/Wishart alternative uses 200 Gaussian null correlation matrices, correlation/PCA eigenvalues and the same 95th-percentile rule while retaining a zero-factor suggestion. Exact Wishart sampling with N−1 degrees of freedom avoids an N-by-items allocation and bounds its work independently of sample size. Older sessions retain this method and their original seed. The reference common-factor method generates the actual null observations in column order and has a separate on-device work limit; it does not silently replace or drop simulations. Null preliminary boundary/nonconvergence spectra remain in the selected reference protocol and are disclosed. Tables show both observed eigenvalue bases. Scree curves and their null thresholds share the selected basis, including negative common-factor values.

Counts are capped at six and must have nonnegative nominal model degrees of freedom: `p(p+1)/2 − [pm + p − m(m−1)/2] ≥ 0`. This condition alone does not establish identification in a particular dataset. Zero suggestions retain the eigenvalue/adequacy outputs without forcing a factor. An automatically suggested count beyond the supported limit is reported without silently truncating it; choose a smaller fixed count explicitly if justified.

KMO and each item's MSA compare squared correlations with squared partial correlations from the inverse correlation matrix. They are unavailable when both sums are zero. Bartlett's sphericity statistic is `−[N−1−(2p+5)/6] log(det(R))`, compared with chi-square on `p(p−1)/2` degrees of freedom. Its approximation assumes multivariate normality. The residual correlation RMSR is the root mean squared off-diagonal difference between observed and reproduced correlations. It is descriptive, not a significance test. KMO, Bartlett, a scree pattern and good residual fit do not establish validity, reliability or a suitable theoretical model. Items are never automatically reversed, removed or imputed.

See [jamovi's EFA options](https://docs.jamovi.org/jmv/jmv_efa.html), [the factor-analyzer method definitions](https://factor-analyzer.readthedocs.io/en/latest/factor_analyzer.html) and [UCLA's factor-analysis guide](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/spss/seminars/efa-spss/).
